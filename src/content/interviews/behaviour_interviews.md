# Behavioral Interviews: Story Selection Over Delivery

## Core Insight
**Story selection matters more than story structure at senior+ levels.
- ** The #1 reason for rejection isn't poor delivery—it's choosing the wrong story.

## The Fundamental Problem

As you become more senior, you accumulate MORE stories but not all showcase the RIGHT level. A Senior Manager telling a line-manager story gives the interviewer no data to justify the higher level. A Staff Engineer sharing a Senior Engineer story signals the wrong scope.

**Key principle:** Your story must show a *delta* from the level below you.

## Data-Backed Reasons for Down-Leveling

All top reasons relate to story selection, not delivery:
- Story lacks appropriate scope/complexity
- Insufficient evidence of impact
- Wrong level of challenge demonstrated
- No clear differentiation from lower level

## The 5-Step Pre-Compute Algorithm

### 1. Brainstorm Stories (with dimensions)

Research what your target company values:
- Handling ambiguity
- Managing conflict
- Driving cross-team initiatives
- Technical trade-offs at scale
- Stakeholder management
- Dealing with failure

**Action:** Brain dump 20-30 stories across all dimensions. Don't filter yet—quantity first. Use AI to ask guiding questions if stuck.

### 2. Identify Scope

For each story, pinpoint what made it HARD:
- Ambiguity level?
- Coordination challenge?
- Cross-functional dependencies?
- Number of people to align?
- Technical complexity?
- Timeline pressure?

**Be specific:** "3-week deadline for typically 3-month project during Q4 code freeze" beats "tight deadline."

### 3. Gather Evidence (THE CRITICAL STEP)

This is where most candidates fail. You need PROOF the challenge was real and impact was meaningful.

**Quantitative Evidence:**
- Team scale: "7 teams across 3 orgs"
- People affected: "45 engineers, 200+ stakeholders"
- Timeline pressure: "6 weeks vs. typical 6 months"
- Financial impact: "$2.3M cost savings"
- Performance: "Reduced P99 latency from 800ms to 50ms"
- Scale metrics: "Handling 10M requests/day"

**Qualitative Evidence:**
- Stakeholder seniority: "VP-level approval required"
- Recognition: "CEO shout-out at all-hands"
- Customer testimonials: "This saved our Black Friday launch"
- Career impact: Promotion or expanded scope afterward
- Industry impact: Became standard adopted by other teams
- Failure recovery: "Initially rejected by 4 teams, eventually adopted company-wide"

**Where to find it:** Old emails, Slack messages, performance reviews, design docs.

### 4. Filter Ruthlessly

**Delete any story lacking BOTH clear challenges AND compelling evidence at your target level.**

Examples to DELETE:
- Debugged tricky issue (unless saved millions or unblocked 50+ engineers)
- Helped onboard teammate (unless they became tech lead in record time)
- Project that went smoothly (no conflict = no story)

**Goal:** End with 6-10 stories that make interviewers think "Wow, that IS hard" or "I've never dealt with that complexity."

### 5. Craft Your Title

Create a hook that includes a hint of evidence.

- ❌ "Let me tell you about a time I resolved conflict"
- ✅ "Let me tell you about the time two VPs wanted opposite architectures, and I had 3 weeks to align them"

- ❌ "Let me tell you about a project I led"
- ✅ "Let me tell you about leading a migration that 4 teams said was impossible—until we did it in 6 weeks"

## Real Examples

### Meta - Staff Level Conflict Resolution

**Rejected Stories:**
- Disagreed with PM on roadmap (just 2 conversations, no escalation)
- Architect vs. me on microservices (single team, clear ownership)
- Security blocked launch (standard approval, no ambiguity)

**Story That Survived:**
*"Two teams built competing APIs for 3 months, burning $3M, until I got them in the same room."*

**The Challenge:**
- 12 engineers across Payment and Identity orgs
- Both APIs technically valid with existing dependencies
- 6-month timeline at risk
- No clear decision maker
- Both leads refused joint meetings

**The Evidence:**
- $3M budget at risk
- Would affect 15 downstream services
- Required director-level escalation
- Became template for future API governance
- Saved 4 months of parallel development

### Amazon - Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit (Staff Level)

**Rejected Stories:**
- Pushed back on director's architecture (they changed decision after one meeting—no real disagreement)
- Disagreed with team on build vs buy (majority ruled, I went along—that's agreement)
- Questioned quarterly roadmap (withdrew when I had no data—not backbone)

**Story That Survived:**
*"I fought my director to save a $1.2M project, lost the argument with data, then helped kill it in record time."*

**The Challenge:**
- Director wanted to sunset my 2-year project
- Usage declining: 5,000 → 1,000 daily users
- I had counter-data (50K users after integration)
- Team morale at stake
- Would affect 4 teams, 15 engineers

**The Evidence:**
- $1.2M invested
- Requested 30 days to prove value with metrics
- Lost with data showing my projections too optimistic
- After being overruled: helped deprecate in 2 weeks (not planned 2 months)
- Volunteered to lead sunsetting and stakeholder communication

## FAQ Quick Reference

**Q: What if I don't have enough evidence?**
1. Dig deeper (old emails, Slack, perf reviews)
2. Start collecting now for future
3. Acknowledge the gap—you might not have Staff-level experience yet

**Q: How specific should evidence be?**
Very. "Large team" → "27 engineers across 4 teams." Precise numbers feel real; round numbers feel made up.

**Q: Can I use same story for multiple dimensions?**
Yes, with different evidence for each. A migration might show:
- Leadership: Aligned 15 engineers across 3 teams
- Ambiguity: Requirements changed 4 times mid-project
- Technical excellence: Zero-downtime migration of 10TB

**Q: What if evidence is confidential?**
Anonymize thoughtfully: "Saved 7 figures annually" or "Reduced costs by ~70%." Magnitude matters more than specifics.

**Q: How many stories do I need?**
6-10 stories with strong evidence, each showing different dimensions with 3-5 solid proof points.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Story selection > Story structure** at senior+ levels
2. **Evidence is everything**—if you can't prove it, you can't use it
3. **Be ruthless in filtering**—6-10 great stories beat 30 mediocre ones
4. **Level-appropriate challenges**—show the delta from the level below
5. **If nothing survives the filter**—you may need to target a different level or create better experiences first
